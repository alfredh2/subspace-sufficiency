## 2. Reassessment of the direction
The seven phases support a narrower statement than the project's motivating one: *for one late-layer accept/reject readout, a few hundred native MLP units and a handful of late heads carry the write into a shared direction; the same components are not verdict-specific, and the d_c-aligned part of an upstream write can be causally minor.* Three competing readings of that result must be separated before generalising:
* **H-native** (the project's premise): behaviour-relevant low-dimensional representations are written by sparse, behaviour-selective native components, more than a generic basis would give.
* **H-generic**: native-basis concentration of the write into *any* direction (including behaviourally irrelevant ones) is large because of heavy-tailed activations, outlier dimensions and gains in the native basis; it says little about behaviour. Existing evidence against it: shuffled-alignment (units) and activation-/alignment-matched controls (causal). Still untested for random target directions and shuffled class labels.
* **H-readout**: what is concentrated is a *shared late-layer decision/answer-formatting readout* (accept/reject, answer-token routing), reused by many tasks; task-specific computation is elsewhere, and the native components are general-purpose.
Phase 7 is designed so that each reading makes a different prediction (Section 6) and so that concentration, causal control and selectivity are reported as three separate properties.

## 3. Definitions and comparable measures (fixed now)
* **Coordinate concentration (descriptive alignment).** For a task with target direction d (residual index of its hook) and behavioural classes (+, −): coordinate j (an MLP unit, or a head block) contributes Δ_j = (ā_j^+ − ā_j^−)·g_j with g = Wᵀd (exact additive decomposition of the class difference of d·h). **All concentration statistics are computed on held-out data after the ranking is frozen on training data**, and in rotated/null bases the *same* train-rank-then-test-evaluate pipeline is applied. Primary: (a) held-out top-q share of Σ|Δ_j| at q = 1 % of the coordinates of the analysed layer set (neurons) or the top 8 of the analysed heads (heads); (b) normalised effective number N_eff/n, N_eff = 1/Σp_j², p = |Δ|/Σ|Δ|; (c) held-out signed reconstruction R(k) = Σ_{top-k} Δ_j / Σ_j Δ_j and k90 = smallest k with R(k) ≥ 0.9. Gini and entropy only as supplements. **Comparisons are only made within the same coordinate system against its own nulls (ratio, z, empirical rank), or through k90 expressed in coordinates and in parameters; neurons (12,288 coordinates per layer) and heads (32 blocks of 128) are never compared by raw shares.**
* **Nulls for concentration** (each reported separately, never pooled): true Haar rotations of the post-gate activation space per layer (n = 20; exact two-frame sampler for n = 1000), block-Haar for heads, structured permutations, shuffled alignment (permute g across coordinates), **new:** (i) *label-shuffled* class difference (random class assignment, 50 draws), (ii) *random target direction* of matched norm and (iii) *behaviour-irrelevant directions* (top residual PCs; the unembedding difference of two arbitrary token pairs), evaluated with the same labels; **new data-adapted bases:** PCA basis of the activations, an ICA/whitened basis and an **optimised sparse rotation** (rotation maximising train concentration of Δ; evaluated held-out), which give an *upper reference* for what any rotation could reach.
* **Causal control.** The effect of intervening on the frozen top-k coordinates on the task behaviour: ablation (necessity), counterfactual transplantation between matched pairs (sufficiency, where pairs exist), and dose-response (k grid, patch α grid). Effect sizes are reported raw (logits, AUC, accuracy) and as fractions of two references: R_dir = effect of ablating the task's MVS consensus direction, and R_set = effect of ablating *all* units/heads of the analysed layer set (does not presuppose d). Controls: random, activation-matched, alignment-matched, magnitude-matched, rotated-top (same selection procedure in the rotated basis), PCA-top, optimised-rotation-top, label-shuffled-selected and random-direction-selected sets, compared **at matched perturbation size** (effect against ‖Δh‖ curves; clamped extrapolation is flagged).
* **Ranking validity (new).** Alignment rank vs causal rank: Spearman and precision@k between |Δ_j| (train) and single-component causal effects (attribution patching, then direct ablation of the top candidates); this makes explicit when concentration of alignment does *not* predict causal control (Phase 2: ρ = 0.36).
* **Causal selectivity.** The cross-task matrix E_ij (effect of task i's frozen set on task j's behaviour, each with its own controls), the specificity index SI_i = (E_ii − median_{j≠i} E_ij)/E_ii, within-group versus across-group contrasts for pre-specified task groups, component-set overlap versus chance (hypergeometric), and off-target side effects (WikiText KL; verdict-free tasks; reported against the control-draw range, since fixed 1-logit limits failed in Phases 1–2 even for generic perturbations).

## 5. Staged plan
**Stage 0 — infrastructure, regression and falsification checks (0.5 GPU-h).** Generic task interface (prompt builder, answer-token sets per example, label formats, matched-pair builder, train/val/test split by lexical content, three generator seeds); regression gate: the new generic pipeline must reproduce the Phase 1/2 logic numbers (top-1 % share 0.490, reconstruction 0.984, S8 share 0.778) within 0.01. **Falsification gate (cheap, uses existing logic data):** concentration of the *same* class-difference decomposition for (i) shuffled labels, (ii) random target directions, (iii) top residual PCs and unembedding-difference directions of unrelated token pairs, (iv) PCA / ICA / optimised rotations. If random or irrelevant targets reach ≥ 0.7 of the native top-1 % share seen for d_c, H-generic is supported for the geometry and the concentration claim is downgraded before any scale-up.

**Stage M — small methodological experiment on verdict-aligned versus orthogonal contributions (≈ 0.7 GPU-h; recommended before the Phase 7 ranking criteria are frozen).** *Why it is warranted:* Phase 6 compared the d_c-aligned component of the layer-19 write at the summary rows (T_free 0.024) with the remaining orthogonal component (T_free 0.27), but (a) the two components have different norms and no norm-matched or dose-matched comparison was made, so "d_c-aligned is inert" may be a magnitude effect; (b) d_c is the *answer-position* direction at index 23, not necessarily the direction through which summary rows pass messages that attention later reads (OV circuits change direction), so orthogonality to d_c at another position/layer is expected rather than paradoxical; (c) it bears directly on Phase 7's central choice, namely ranking components by contribution to d (alignment) versus by causal effect. *Design (reusing `phase6` dcomp and `residual_set_patch` delta machinery):* decompose Δh(20) at rows G1∪G2 into d_c, an effect-optimal direction g (average gradient of the margin with respect to the residual at those rows, estimated on validation contents and frozen), the span of the top PCs of Δh, and the remainder; for each, patch at natural magnitude, rescaled to the norm of the full Δ, and against equal-norm random directions inside the orthogonal complement (n = 20); free and pinned (layers 20–22). Predictions: M-A the earlier finding is a magnitude artefact (scaled d_c-only reaches ≥ 0.5 of the orthogonal effect); M-B the orthogonal component is special (actual orthogonal effect ≥ 0.5 of full and equal-norm random orthogonal directions ≤ 0.1 of it, while scaled d_c-only ≤ 0.1); M-C a position-specific direction g carries ≥ 0.7 of the effect with a small cosine to d_c (d_c is the wrong direction for this position). Outcome selects the Phase 7 default: alignment to d_c only (M-A), or alignment plus attribution-based causal ranking as co-primary (M-B/M-C). Any outcome is reported; it cannot change earlier verdicts.

**Stage 1 — task feasibility and target representations (≈ 4 GPU-h).** For each candidate: baseline accuracy ≥ 0.9 (categorical: ≥ 0.8) in every format; MVS sweep (hooks {19, 21, 23, 25}, ranks {1, 2, 4}, 300 steps each, ≈ 2 min) with a frozen choice rule (smallest rank ≤ 4 and the earliest hook whose retained-KL ≤ 0.05 and whose ablation reduces the margin class gap by ≥ 50 %, minimum 10 logits); final MVS per (task, format) with three seeds (1600 steps, ≈ 6 min each; consensus direction via principal angles as in Phase 0.5, kept only if cross-seed cosine ≥ 0.7); format-pooled MVS as a secondary test (Phase 0.5 found retention format-bound). Tasks failing the gate are dropped (reported). Deliverable: per task a consensus direction d_task, hook, rank, validity floors.

**Stage 2 — coordinate concentration (≈ 2 GPU-h).** Layer sets by the Phase 1 rule (cumulative mass 99.5 % of Σ|D^mlp|), per task, neurons and heads: the measures and nulls of Section 3 on held-out content, three generator seeds, three MVS seeds. Output: concentration table, native / each null / optimised-rotation reference; k90 in coordinates and parameters; cross-seed stability of the selected sets.

**Stage 3 — causal control (≈ 6 GPU-h).** Frozen k: units 1 % of the analysed layer set (plus a k grid), heads 8 (plus nested). (a) alignment-ranked and attribution-ranked sets (attribution patching on train, verified by direct ablation of the top candidates); (b) ablation, mean and resample replacement; (c) transplantation between matched pairs; (d) dose-response; (e) all controls of Section 3 at matched perturbation size with n = 20 draws per family (empirical p floor 1/21 stated; z-scores with Holm correction); (f) the ranking-validity statistics. For attention heads the Phase 2.5 lesson applies: use model-native `z` interventions; any recompute-based decomposition needs its own acceptance gate first.

**Stage 4 — selectivity and format generality (≈ 4 GPU-h).** Cross-task matrix for all frozen sets (units and heads), overlap versus chance, and a **format factorial** for the two binary tasks L and S: answer formats {digits, letters, words} × {original, reversed} and the same sets evaluated in every format; the question is whether the sets track the *semantic verdict* (reversed mapping behaves semantically, cross-format transfer of the set) or the *answer token*. Task groups pre-specified: verdict-type {L, S} versus non-verdict {G, N, M}.

**Stage 5 — SAE / learned-dictionary comparison (2 GPU-h with public SAEs, ≈ 8 more if one must be trained).** *Availability (not yet verified by me beyond a web search):* a recent paper (Qwen-Scope, arXiv 2605.11887) reports residual-stream SAEs for Qwen3-8B **Base** (64K latents, TopK 50 / 100, all 36 layers); our model is the post-trained `Qwen/Qwen3-8B`, so base-to-post-trained transfer must be checked and repository ids, licence and loaders confirmed. *Fair comparison, because a sparse dictionary is not a rotation of the neuron basis:* (1) **same target and same data**: the class difference of d·h at the hook, with an explicit reconstruction-error ("dark matter") term kept in every intervention; (2) SAE gating checks on our activations (variance explained, CE loss recovered, dead/dense latents) before any use; (3) **matched budgets**: k90 of held-out class-difference reconstruction and causal efficiency E(k) (and per ‖Δh‖) for SAE latents versus native units, native units + heads, PCA, optimised rotation and shuffled/random dictionaries of the same width; (4) widths compared at several sizes, because per-feature sparsity grows with dictionary size; native units (12,288 per layer) are compared with SAE widths of the same order and larger; (5) **decomposition mismatch stated**: native units decompose the *update* of one layer, SAE latents decompose the *accumulated* residual state, so the native decomposition is evaluated on the layer-set sum (plus heads) and the SAE on the total at the hook, and the comparison is restricted to the shared quantity (class difference of d·h and the behaviour); (6) selectivity (cross-task) and stability (across SAE seeds, impossible for native units) reported but not mixed into the efficiency contest. Outcomes are descriptive (native better / comparable / worse / not comparable) with CIs; no claim that "neurons beat SAEs" is made from efficiency alone.

**Stage 6 — replication (≈ 5 GPU-h).** Fresh generator seeds with freshly generated lexicons (frozen manifest before any intervention, as for set C in Phase 6), and an **optional second model** (Qwen3-4B, if its weights are available locally) on the L, S and one non-verdict task with the same frozen procedures. No tuning on replication data.

## 6. Hypotheses, predictions and preregistered criteria
Panel T* = tasks passing the Stage-1 gate (|T*| ≥ 4 including L required for generalisation claims; otherwise the claims are limited to the passing tasks and labelled "insufficient panel"). All criteria are evaluated per task on held-out content, then counted over tasks; pair/content-clustered intervals as in Phases 4–6; frozen ranking, k and thresholds; failures reported literally.
* **H1 coordinate concentration (neurons and heads separately).** Supported for a task if, for the held-out statistics: top-q share ratio vs true/block Haar ≥ 2 and above every draw; N_eff null/native ≥ 5; k90 native ≤ 0.5 × k90 Haar; **and** vs the marginal-controlled family (shuffled alignment, shuffled labels, random and irrelevant target directions) ratio ≥ 1.3 and above ≥ 95 % of draws; held-out reconstruction by the frozen top 1 % / top 8 heads ≥ 0.6 with random sets ≤ 0.2. *H-generic prediction:* the marginal-controlled family reaches ≥ 0.7 of native. Overall: supported if ≥ 4 tasks pass for neurons (heads reported separately); mixed if 2–3; failure if ≤ 1 or if Stage-0 falsification supports H-generic.
* **H2 causal control.** For a task: E_native ≥ 0.30 of R_dir and of R_set; exceeds the best matched control by ≥ 0.15 and every draw of random/activation-/alignment-matched families; exceeds rotated-top and PCA-top by ≥ 0.15 at matched ‖Δh‖; dose-response monotone (Spearman ≥ 0.8); transplantation transfer ≥ 0.25 where pairs exist; ranking validity: Spearman(alignment, causal) ≥ 0.5 and precision@k ≥ 0.5 (if not, "concentration of alignment does not predict causal control" is reported as the finding). Overall as for H1.
* **H3 selectivity.** On-target E_ii ≥ 0.30 and SI_i ≥ 0.5 for task i = *task-specific*; off-target within-group E ≥ 0.5·E_ii but across-group SI ≥ 0.5 = *channel-specific*; median off-target E ≥ 0.5·E_ii = *general-purpose*; side effects judged against the control-draw range (native shift ≤ the 95th percentile of equal-size random sets) and WikiText KL ≤ 0.02. Reported as a classification of each frozen set, and of the panel (e.g. "units are channel-specific, heads general-purpose"); no pass/fail on a single number.
* **H4 generality beyond verdict and answer token.** Supported if H1 and H2 hold for ≥ 2 non-verdict tasks (G, N, M) **and** for L/S in ≥ 2 answer formats with the format-factorial pattern showing semantic rather than token-bound control (reversed mapping follows the verdict; cross-format set overlap ≥ 5× chance and cross-format E ≥ 0.2). Mixed if only verdict-type tasks pass; failure if neither.
* **H5 SAE comparison.** Pre-specified outcomes on k90 and E(k) at matched k (primary: area under E(k) for k ≤ k_max, intervals by paired bootstrap): native better if its area exceeds the SAE's by ≥ 0.1 with CI excluding 0 for ≥ 3 tasks; comparable if |difference| < 0.1; SAE better if the converse; SAE unusable if its gating checks fail (then a self-trained SAE is the only fair comparator or the comparison is dropped).
* **Overall labels.** SUPPORTED (H-native): H1 ∧ H2 ∧ H3 (task- or channel-specific) ∧ H4 ≥ mixed; MIXED: concentration and causal control without selectivity (general-purpose / H-readout), or concentration without predictive causal control; FAILURE: Stage-0 falsification supports H-generic or H1 fails for ≥ 4 tasks. Replication (Stage 6) is decisive when A-type and replication data disagree.

## 7. Major risks and mitigations
1. *Target-representation failure:* MVS may need rank > 2 or no stable consensus for G/N/M. Mitigation: Stage-1 gate, per-contrast scalars for categorical tasks, report drops; do not weaken gates afterwards.
2. *Lexical and format shortcuts (Phase 6):* matched minimal pairs, polarity-neutral lexical swaps, token-identity partitions, reversed mappings and a cross-format design; interpret positives as "verdict-correlated information generalising across tested forms", not abstract competence.
3. *Concentration without behavioural meaning:* handled by the Stage-0 falsification gate and the marginal-controlled nulls; Haar rotations alone are a weak null.
4. *Alignment ≠ causation:* attribution-ranked sets and ranking-validity statistics are co-primary; Stage M clarifies the d_c reference.
5. *Reference dependence:* R_dir presupposes d; R_set and raw effects are co-reported.
6. *Side-effect limits too strict:* judged against equal-size random sets as well as the fixed limits.
7. *Multiplicity and few tasks:* per-task primary contrasts only, Holm within families, k-of-n counting, no pooling of items across tasks; n = 20 draws give p_min = 0.048 and are described as such.
8. *Head-level recomputation errors (Phase 2.5):* native-hook interventions only unless an acceptance gate passes.
9. *SAE mismatch:* base-vs-post-trained SAEs, hook-layer mismatch, decomposition of state vs update; gating checks and explicit comparability statement.
10. *Compute creep:* stage gates (below); the SAE training is the only potentially large item and is gated.

## 11. Approval amendments and preregistered rules for the two pilot experiments (binding)
**Decisions.** (1) Stage M and the Stage-0 falsification checks run first; the report ends there and work **stops for review** before Stage 1 or later. (2) Planned panel: logic (L), sentiment (S), agreement (G), name copying (N) and four-way multiple choice (M), single-digit addition (A) in reserve; chosen by the frozen feasibility screen. (3) Public Qwen-Scope SAEs (Qwen3-8B **Base**, 64K features, TopK 50 / 100, all layers; repository ids, licence and loaders to be confirmed) are the first SAE comparator, **subject to reconstruction and intervention-fidelity checks on the post-trained model** (variance explained, CE-loss recovered, margin and logit KL when the residual is replaced by its reconstruction, dead/dense latents); self-training only with an explicit written justification. (4) Qwen3-4B replication is an explicit **optional, deferred stage** (Stage 6b), considered only after the main multi-task results justify it. (5) The primary question is the three-way contrast, not confirmation of native-neuron specialisation.

**A1 Optimised rotations are an attainable-sparsity benchmark, not a null.** A rotation fitted on training data is never part of a null family or an empirical-p comparison; it is reported separately as the sparsity attainable when the basis is allowed to adapt, and always evaluated on held-out data. Two forms: (a) *single-contrast fit*: a rotation sending the training contrast vector δ and the gain g onto the first coordinates; for one contrast this attains the maximal sparsity analytically (one coordinate carries all of Δ, so the training share is 1) and the held-out share is governed by the cosine between train and test contrasts; this degeneracy is expected and is reported as a limit on what coordinate concentration of a *single* contrast can mean; (b) *transfer fit*: a rotation whose leading coordinates span the contrast vectors of **other** contrasts/tasks, evaluated on the held-out contrast (ranking of coordinates by the evaluation contrast's own training split). The informative question is whether the fixed native basis concentrates many different contrasts at once without fitting; (b) is the benchmark for that.
**A2 The task-by-task causal selectivity matrix is a primary evaluation (H3).** For every frozen set (units, heads, SAE feature sets where available) and every task: E_ij with paired/clustered bootstrap intervals and the same control families for each cell; **negative transfer** (E_ij < 0, a set that *improves* or reverses another task's behaviour) is reported and counted, not clipped; shared-answer-format dependence is tested explicitly by (i) evaluating tasks that share an answer format (digits, letters, words) against tasks that do not, (ii) the format factorial for L and S, and (iii) a token-routing control (ablation effect on the literal answer-token logits independent of the task). Classification (task-specific / channel-specific / format-specific / general-purpose) is made from the matrix, with the within-group versus across-group contrast and the format contrast as separate statistics.
**A3 Behavioural feature-discovery gates (preregistered, applied per task and recorded as outcomes).** A task contributes a target representation only if: (a) held-out baseline accuracy ≥ 0.9 (categorical ≥ 0.8) in every planned format; (b) MVS retention on held-out content: retained-KL ≤ 0.05 **and** retained accuracy ≥ 0.9 × intact; (c) ablation on held-out content reduces the margin class gap by ≥ 50 % and ≥ 10 logits; (d) **held-out causal evidence**: matched-pair transplantation of the discovered subspace transfers ≥ 0.25 of the behavioural gap with random equal-rank subspaces ≤ 0.05; (e) **stability**: cross-seed consensus cosine ≥ 0.7 and, where more than one output format exists, retained and transplantation effects hold in ≥ 2 formats including a reversed mapping (format-pooled MVS reported as a secondary test). A task failing any gate is *recorded as an unsuccessful feature discovery* with the gate values, is not rescued by relaxing a gate, and does not enter the panel; the number of failed tasks is itself reported.
**A4 Geometric and causal rankings are both preserved.** Every ranking is computed and stored for every analysed component set: the geometric (alignment) ranking |Δ_j| (and its signed variant), the attribution-based causal ranking, and, after Stage M, any position-specific direction ranking. Whichever becomes primary, all are carried through Stages 2–4, their rank agreement (Spearman, top-k overlap, precision@k against direct single-component ablation) is a reported result, and **disagreement is treated as a finding, not a nuisance**.
**A5 Matched intervention strength and fair budgets.** Comparisons are made at matched perturbation size (effect vs ‖Δh‖ and vs the number of coordinates, never one without the other); where matched interpolation would extrapolate beyond the control range the comparison is flagged and not used for criteria. SAE feature counts are **never equated with neuron counts**: native units, heads and SAE features are compared through (i) k90 on the same held-out quantity, (ii) causal efficiency E(k) and E per ‖Δh‖, (iii) matched reconstruction error budgets (with the SAE error term kept in every intervention) and (iv) several dictionary widths, and the report states that the decompositions differ (update vs accumulated state, overcomplete vs fixed basis).
**A6 Evidence is counted at the level of tasks.** The unit of replication is the task (and, within a task, the independent content/lexicon and generator seeds), never the number of examples; thousands of examples from one task are one observation of that task. Panel-level claims use k-of-n counting over independent tasks, with intervals from content-clustered bootstrap within tasks; no pooled-example p-values across tasks; if fewer than four tasks pass the gates the claim is restricted and labelled "insufficient panel".
**A7 Freezing.** Before any final evaluation: the task panel and the order of exclusion, the feasibility gates, MVS hooks/ranks, ranking procedures, k grids, control families, thresholds, content/lexicon splits and seeds are written to a frozen manifest with file hashes and committed. No iterative selection on held-out tasks or held-out content; a task that fails a gate is not replaced after seeing evaluation results (the reserve task A is taken only by the pre-stated rule at the Stage-1 screen, before any Stage 2–4 result exists).
